# 12 free courses better than most paid ones 💸

Before you pay for a coding course, look here. All twelve are free to learn from. This is the order I'd do them in, but don't start all twelve: **pick the next one on the list and finish it.**

## Start here: the basics

### 1. CS50x (Harvard)

- **What you learn:** how computers and code actually work, in C, Python, SQL and web basics. The best first course there is.
- **Free:** [cs50.harvard.edu/x](https://cs50.harvard.edu/x/), with lectures, problem sets and a free CS50 certificate if you finish. The edX verified certificate is the paid extra.
- **Start with:** Week 0 (Scratch). It's easier than it looks.

### 2. roadmap.sh

- **What you learn:** what to learn next for the role you want (frontend, backend, DevOps, AI engineer, and more), as a clickable map.
- **Free:** [roadmap.sh](https://roadmap.sh/).
- **Use it for:** deciding between 3 and 4 below, and checking you're not skipping a step.

## Build things for the web

### 3. The Odin Project

- **What you learn:** full-stack web development by building real projects: HTML, CSS, JavaScript, React, Node (or Ruby on Rails).
- **Free:** [theodinproject.com](https://www.theodinproject.com/).
- **Start with:** the Foundations path. Set up your machine the way they say.

### 4. freeCodeCamp

- **What you learn:** web design, JavaScript, Python, data and more, with free certifications and thousands of exercises.
- **Free:** [freecodecamp.org/learn](https://www.freecodecamp.org/learn/).
- **Use it for:** practice between Odin projects.

### 5. Full Stack Open (University of Helsinki)

- **What you learn:** modern web apps with React, Redux, Node.js, MongoDB, GraphQL and TypeScript, the way companies build them.
- **Free:** [fullstackopen.com/en](https://fullstackopen.com/en/), including a free certificate from the University of Helsinki.
- **Do it after:** you're comfortable with JavaScript.

## Crack DSA for placements

### 6. Striver's A2Z DSA Sheet

- **What you learn:** data structures and algorithms in order, from basics to graphs and DP, with a video for most problems.
- **Free:** [the A2Z sheet on takeuforward.org](https://takeuforward.org/prep-hub/strivers-a2z-dsa-sheet).
- **Start with:** step 1, even if it feels too easy.

### 7. NeetCode

- **What you learn:** the patterns behind interview questions, through the NeetCode 150 with video explanations.
- **Free:** [neetcode.io/roadmap](https://neetcode.io/roadmap). NeetCode Pro (extra courses) is paid; the roadmap and its videos are free.
- **Use it for:** revision once Striver's sheet has given you the basics.

### 8. MIT OpenCourseWare

- **What you learn:** real MIT courses with lectures, notes and problem sets. For placements, take 6.006 Introduction to Algorithms.
- **Free:** [ocw.mit.edu](https://ocw.mit.edu/), and [6.006 here](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-006-introduction-to-algorithms-spring-2020/).
- **Do the:** problem sets, not just the videos.

### 9. NPTEL

- **What you learn:** courses by IIT and IISc professors on almost every engineering subject, many of them matching your syllabus.
- **Free:** [nptel.ac.in](https://nptel.ac.in/). Learning is free; the proctored exam for a certificate has a fee.
- **Use it for:** the subject your college teaches worst.

## Get into AI and data

### 10. Kaggle Learn

- **What you learn:** Python, pandas, SQL, machine learning and more, in short hands-on courses you finish in an evening.
- **Free:** [kaggle.com/learn](https://www.kaggle.com/learn), with a free certificate for each course.
- **Start with:** Intro to Machine Learning.

### 11. fast.ai: Practical Deep Learning for Coders

- **What you learn:** how to train real deep-learning models first, then how they work underneath.
- **Free:** [course.fast.ai](https://course.fast.ai/).
- **You need:** about a year of Python.

## Want the whole degree?

### 12. OSSU Computer Science

- **What it is:** a complete computer-science curriculum built only from free online courses, in the order a CS degree would teach them.
- **Free:** [github.com/ossu/computer-science](https://github.com/ossu/computer-science).
- **Use it for:** filling the gaps your college syllabus leaves.

---

**How to actually finish one:** block 5 hours a week, code along instead of watching, and push what you build to GitHub. One finished course plus a project beats twelve half-watched playlists.
