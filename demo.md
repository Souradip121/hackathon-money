# Demo day checklist ✅

At Hack4Bengal 3.0 my team didn't sleep, still made it to round two, and then fumbled in front of the judges. We couldn't answer their questions. Your code doesn't get tired. Your pitch does.

Hackathons aren't won by the team that never sleeps. They're won by the team that **sleeps in shifts**.

## 1. Plan the sleep (night one)

- [ ] Sleep in shifts: two people awake at a time, never the whole team at 4 AM.
- [ ] **The presenter sleeps first**, and gets at least 4 hours before judging.
- [ ] Set alarms for every shift change and for the submission deadline.
- [ ] Eat a real meal before the last stretch. Go easy on energy drinks after midnight.

## 2. Freeze the demo (3–4 hours before judging)

- [ ] Stop adding features. Fix only what breaks the main flow.
- [ ] Write the demo script: one flow, start to finish, the same clicks every time.
- [ ] Load test data so the demo never shows an empty screen.
- [ ] Record a 60–90 second **backup demo video** and keep it offline on the laptop and a phone.
- [ ] Note your live link and keep it open in a tab.

## 3. Rehearse (2 hours before)

- [ ] Run the full pitch out loud, with a timer, at least twice.
- [ ] Practise the handover: who talks, who clicks.
- [ ] List the 5 questions judges will ask and agree on one-line answers:
  - What problem does it solve, and for whom?
  - What did you build during the hackathon?
  - What's the hardest technical part?
  - How would you scale it or make money?
  - Why your approach and not an existing tool?

## 4. Right before you present

- [ ] Laptop charged, charger in the bag, notifications off.
- [ ] Phone hotspot ready in case the venue Wi-Fi dies.
- [ ] Backup video one click away.
- [ ] **The 10-second rule:** if the live demo stalls for 10 seconds, switch to the video calmly. Never debug in front of the judges.

## 5. After you present

- [ ] Note the judges' questions. They tell you what to fix for the next round.
- [ ] Thank the sponsor judges and connect on LinkedIn.
