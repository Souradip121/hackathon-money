# SIH Finale Kit (the one I wish I'd had) 🏆

I won SIH 2024, and I've mentored or judged at nine hackathons. Same lesson every time: teams rarely lose on code. They lose on the pitch, the demo, or a dead hotspot at 3 a.m.

Here's everything I'd hand a shortlisted team.

## SIH 2026 basics (checked 20 Oct 2026)

- **Finale:** offline, at nodal centres across India. Planned for **December 2026**. Exact dates aren't out yet, so watch [sih.gov.in](https://sih.gov.in/).
- **Format:** the 2025 software finale ran **36 hours non-stop**. Confirm the 2026 format for your centre.
- **Team:** 6 students from the same college, with at least one woman. Up to 2 mentors can come too.
- **Prize:** **₹1.5 lakh per problem statement** (2026 guidelines). That's ₹25,000 each for 6. In 2024 it was ₹1 lakh, about ₹16,700 each.
- **The fine print:** 4–5 teams per problem statement may reach the finale. The organisation that set the problem pays only if it likes the winning idea, and it doesn't have to declare a winner at all.

## The 3-minute pitch

| Time | What you say |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | **The problem as one person's story.** "This is Meena. Every week she…" One person, one pain. No statistics yet. |
| 0:30 | **Live demo, one flow.** Show Meena's problem getting solved, start to finish. One flow only, not a feature tour. |
| 2:00 | **Impact.** Who uses it, how many people, what it costs, and what changes. Use numbers from the problem statement or a source you can name. |
| 2:30 | **Team and what's next.** Who built what, in one line each. Then your pilot plan with the ministry. |
| 3:00 | **Stop.** Even mid-sentence. Going over time looks unprepared. |

Rehearse it 5 times with a timer. The person demoing should not also be the person talking.

## Questions judges actually ask

Assign each question to one teammate before the finale. Answer in 2–3 sentences.

1. Who will actually use this? Have you spoken to one of them?
2. Does it work offline or on a weak 2G/3G network?
3. Why not just use ChatGPT for this?
4. Where does your data come from? Is it real or dummy?
5. What does it cost to run, per user per month?
6. How does it scale to a whole state, or 1 crore users?
7. What already exists, like a government app or portal? How is yours better?
8. How will the ministry or department actually deploy this?
9. How do you protect personal data? (Know the basics of the DPDP Act.)
10. How accurate is it? How did you test that?
11. What happens when the model or the sensor is wrong?
12. Does it work in Hindi or regional languages?
13. What did you build here, and what did you bring ready-made?
14. Who maintains it after the hackathon?
15. What would you do in the next 3 months with support?

"We don't know yet, here's how we'd find out" beats bluffing. Every single time.

## The 36-hour plan (6-hour blocks)

Judging rounds and timings differ by nodal centre. Ask at check-in and pin the schedule somewhere everyone can see it.

| Hours | Focus |
|---|---|
| 0–6 | **Lock the scope.** Pick the one demo flow. Split roles: 2 core devs, 1 frontend, 1 data/ML, 1 pitch and slides, 1 testing and backups. Meet your problem-statement mentor early and ask what they really want. |
| 6–12 | **Build the flow end to end.** Ugly but working beats pretty but half done. Commit often. |
| 12–18 | **Night 1: sleep in shifts.** Three sleep for about 3 hours while three build, then swap. Nobody skips sleep. |
| 18–24 | **Make it real.** Real or realistic data, the offline flow, error states. Start the slides. |
| 24–30 | **Freeze features.** Record the backup demo video. Second sleep shift (about 90 minutes each). |
| 30–36 | **Rehearse.** Pitch 5 times and run the judge Q&A drill. Fix bugs only, no new features. |

## Demo backup plan

1. **Recorded video:** a 60–90 second screen recording of the exact demo flow. Keep copies on 2 laptops, a phone and a pen drive.
2. **Offline flow:** load local seed data and run a local server or APK. Cache API or model responses for your demo inputs.
3. **Hotspot:** carry two phones on two different networks, both recharged with data.
4. **Screenshots:** put the key screens on your last slides.
5. **The 10-second rule:** if the live demo stalls for 10 seconds, calmly switch to the video. Never debug in front of the judges.

## Packing list

- College photo ID (stamped by your college) and a copy of the consent letter. The 2026 guidelines say both are mandatory
- College authorisation letter on letterhead, naming all 6 members and your mentors
- Aadhaar or another government ID
- Laptops and their chargers
- Extension board (4+ sockets) with a long cord
- Power banks and phone cables
- Phone hotspot plus a backup data pack
- HDMI and USB-C adapters
- Pen drive with the backup video and slides
- Earphones, notebook and pens
- Water bottle, snacks and any medicines you need
- A light jacket (halls get cold at night)
- Toothbrush, towel and a change of clothes
- Every travel ticket and bill (for your reimbursement claim)

## Travel claim (verify for your centre)

The SIH 2026 guidelines say:

- Shortlisted students and their mentor can claim travel reimbursement of **up to ₹3,000 per person** for the round trip, local travel included, at actual cost.
- You must show the actual bills. Any mode of travel counts.
- Nodal centres arrange accommodation during the finale.
- Only registered team members and mentors can stay at the venue.

Keep every ticket, auto or cab receipt and booking email. Photograph them the same day.
