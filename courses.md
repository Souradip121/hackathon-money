# 7 free courses your syllabus skips 🎓

All seven are free, from MIT, Berkeley, CMU, Google and Andrej Karpathy. This is the order I'd do them in. Don't collect all seven: **pick ONE this month and finish it.**

## 1. CS 61A: programming fundamentals (UC Berkeley)

- **What you learn:** programming the Berkeley way, in Python (with some SQL), from functions to interpreters.
- **Free:** the textbook [Composing Programs, 3rd edition](https://www.composingprograms.com/3ed/) and the [Fall 2026 course site](https://cs61a.org/fa26/) with lecture videos, homework, labs and projects. Some sections and recordings are Berkeley-only.
- **Start with:** chapter 1 of the textbook and the first lab.

## 2. The Missing Semester of Your CS Education (MIT)

- **What you learn:** the tools your degree assumes you already know: the shell, Git, your dev environment, debugging, packaging and shipping code, and agentic coding.
- **Free:** [missing.csail.mit.edu](https://missing.csail.mit.edu/). The 2026 edition is 9 lectures on YouTube.
- **Start with:** the Shell and Git lectures. They pay off the same week.

## 3. 6.006 Introduction to Algorithms (MIT OpenCourseWare)

- **What you learn:** data structures, sorting, graphs, shortest paths and dynamic programming. This is what placement interviews ask.
- **Free:** [the Spring 2020 course on OCW](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-006-introduction-to-algorithms-spring-2020/), with 21 lecture videos, notes, and problem sets and exams **with solutions**.
- **Start with:** do the problem sets, not just the videos.

## 4. nand2tetris: build a computer from first principles

- **What you learn:** you build a computer from NAND gates up: hardware, assembler, virtual machine, compiler, and an OS that runs Tetris.
- **Free:** lectures, projects and tools at [nand2tetris.org](https://www.nand2tetris.org/). On Coursera, [Part I](https://www.coursera.org/learn/build-a-computer) and [Part II](https://www.coursera.org/learn/nand2tetris2) are free to audit; graded work and the certificate are paid.
- **Start with:** Project 1. You'll build your first logic gates in an evening.

## 5. 15-445/645 Database Systems (Carnegie Mellon)

- **What you learn:** how a database works inside, by building one (BusTub) across 4 projects.
- **Free:** the [Fall 2025 lectures by Andy Pavlo](https://15445.courses.cs.cmu.edu/fall2025/) on YouTube. The Fall 2026 lectures are CMU-only, and the [course FAQ](https://15445.courses.cs.cmu.edu/fall2026/faq.html) points everyone else to Fall 2025. The project code is on GitHub, and there's a public Gradescope (the FAQ has the entry code). Don't publish your solutions.
- **Start with:** Project 0 (the C++ primer).

## 6. Machine Learning Crash Course (Google)

- **What you learn:** ML models, data, embeddings and LLMs, plus real-world ML and fairness.
- **Free:** [developers.google.com/machine-learning/crash-course](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/crash-course). 12 modules, 130+ exercises, a badge per module.
- **You need:** Python, NumPy, pandas and basic maths.

## 7. Neural Networks: Zero to Hero (Andrej Karpathy)

- **What you learn:** from backpropagation to building GPT and its tokenizer from scratch, in Python.
- **Free:** [karpathy.ai/zero-to-hero.html](https://karpathy.ai/zero-to-hero.html). 8 lectures, about 14.5 hours.
- **You need:** Python and basic calculus. Do it after #6.

---

**How to actually finish one:** block 5 hours a week in your calendar, code along instead of watching, and post what you built at the end. A finished course plus a project on GitHub beats seven half-watched playlists.
